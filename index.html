🖍️ The Giant Floor Mural20–45 min · Low–medium mess · 5 store items ⌄

Tape down a huge piece of paper and let her draw with her whole body instead of staying inside a little page.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Hard floor or large table
  • Optional toy animals/cars for tracing

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Roll out 5–8 feet of paper and tape every edge flat.

  2. 2

    Put out only 6–10 markers/crayons at first.

  3. 3

    Draw one road, circle, house, or giant squiggle to invite her in — then let her take over.

  4. 4

    Add dot markers halfway through so the activity feels new again.

  5. 5

    Write the date and let her add one final giant mark before you roll it up.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Trace her body outline and let her color inside it.
  • Draw roads and parking spaces for toy cars.
  • Make one mural every month and compare them over time.
  • Cut one section into greeting cards or hang a favorite section at toddler eye level.
🔴 Dot Marker Rainbow Hunt10–25 min · Low mess · 3 store items ⌄

A very easy, very satisfying color game using big washable dot markers.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Masking/painter’s tape
  • A few household objects in different colors

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Make six large circles or simple blobs on the paper.

  2. 2

    Ask her to fill each area with dots — matching colors if she wants, random if she does not.

  3. 3

    Place a red toy, blue cup, yellow block, etc. nearby and play find-that-color.

  4. 4

    Let dots overlap to discover new color combinations.

  5. 5

    Stop while she is still having fun rather than trying to finish the sheet.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Make a giant rainbow on easel paper.
  • Dot along the first letter of her name.
  • Use finished pages as wrapping paper for family gifts.
  • Put the best sheet in a simple frame.
🦊 Wooden Animal Painting Workshop25–45 min + dry · Medium mess · 7 store items ⌄

Paint a few chunky unfinished wooden animals or shapes, then use them later for pretend play.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Cookie sheet or cardboard drying surface

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Choose only 2–4 wooden pieces for one session.

  2. 2

    Put small puddles of 3–5 paint colors on a palette.

  3. 3

    Let her paint every side she can reach. There is no wrong color.

  4. 4

    Dry on a tray; flip later and paint the underside in a second session.

  5. 5

    If you want the piece to last, an adult can seal it after the paint is completely dry.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Paint a set of animals that becomes her own play collection.
  • Paint a wooden box to store the finished animals.
  • Write the date underneath.
  • Photograph her holding each piece before it gets played with.
🖼️ Tape-Resist Canvas20–35 min + dry · Medium mess · 6 store items ⌄

You make simple tape lines; she paints everything. Peeling the tape gives a dramatic reveal.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Tweezers or fingernail to lift tape after drying

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Before she starts, press painter’s tape across the canvas in a few big lines or make a simple letter.

  2. 2

    Let her cover as much of the canvas as she wants.

  3. 3

    Use a foam brush if she wants to cover large areas quickly.

  4. 4

    Let the canvas dry.

  5. 5

    Adult slowly peels the tape away for the reveal.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Tape the first letter of her name.
  • Do three canvases with different color families.
  • Make a pair: one for her room and one as a grandparent gift.
  • Repeat yearly using the same tape pattern.
🚗 Toy Car Paint Tracks15–30 min · Medium–high mess · 4 store items ⌄

Turn washable paint into a racetrack and make wheel patterns across a giant sheet.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Washable plastic toy cars with chunky wheels
  • Tub or sink ready for immediate car washing

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Tape a long strip of paper to the floor.

  2. 2

    Put a very thin layer of washable paint in a shallow tray.

  3. 3

    Roll one car through paint, then across the paper.

  4. 4

    Try two colors on different cars and cross the tracks.

  5. 5

    Put cars directly into the sink/tub before the paint dries.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Draw roads with marker first.
  • Use different tire textures and compare the tracks.
  • Make a finish line and race two colors.
  • Cut the coolest tire-track section into a long wall strip.
🦕 Dinosaur + Animal Footprints15–30 min · Medium mess · 4 store items ⌄

Plastic animals become giant stamps. Great for cause-and-effect and pretend play.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Plastic dinosaurs/animals with washable feet
  • Old toothbrush or scrub brush for cleanup

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Put a thin puddle of paint on the palette.

  2. 2

    Dip or brush paint onto the animal feet.

  3. 3

    Make a trail of prints across the page.

  4. 4

🌈 Secret Crayon + Watercolor15–25 min · Medium mess · 4 store items ⌄

White crayon marks look invisible until watercolor washes over them.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Water cup
  • Paper towel

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Adult draws a few simple white crayon shapes, lines, or her initial.

  2. 2

    Let her add her own white crayon scribbles.

  3. 3

    Brush watercolor over the paper.

  4. 4

    Watch the wax resist the paint and reveal the hidden marks.

  5. 5

    Try a second sheet where she chooses the watercolor colors.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Draw hearts, stars, animals, or her name invisibly.
  • Use multiple crayon colors under watercolor.
  • Make secret birthday cards.
  • Write what the hidden drawing was on the back.
🟨 Sponge Shape Printing20–30 min · Medium mess · 5 store items ⌄

Big sponges make easy stamps without tiny craft pieces.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Old tray or baking sheet

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Adult cuts sponges into BIG circles, squares, hearts, or triangles.

  2. 2

    Add a thin layer of paint to a tray.

  3. 3

    Press sponge into paint, then stamp the paper.

  4. 4

    Overlap shapes and colors.

  5. 5

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Build a house from stamped squares and triangles.
  • Make a field of flowers.

  • Keep the sponge set in a labeled zip bag.
🍃 Nature Stamp Lab25–40 min · Medium mess · 4 store items ⌄

Collect leaves and other large natural textures outside, then print them with washable paint.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Large leaves, pinecones, flat bark pieces
  • Basket for collecting

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Collect a few large, clean nature items together.

  2. 2

    Brush a small amount of paint onto one textured surface.

  3. 3

    Press it onto paper and lift straight up.

  4. 4

    Try the opposite side or a second color.

  5. 5

    Talk about which objects make lines, dots, veins, or blobs.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Make a seasonal page.
  • Use a long paper roll and create a nature border.
  • Photograph the original leaf next to its print.
  • Paint around a leaf rather than stamping it.

Squish, roll, stamp, poke, and compare textures.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

DO THIS

  2. 2

  3. 3

🖐️ Handprint Family Canvas15–25 min + dry · Medium–high mess · 5 store items ⌄

A controlled messy project that creates a keepsake instead of just another sheet of paper.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Wet washcloth ready before you start

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Choose one paint color and brush a thin layer onto her palm.

  2. 2

    Press hand straight down onto the canvas and lift straight up.

  3. 3

    Wash her hand immediately.

  4. 4

    After it dries, add another handprint or let her paint around it.

  5. 5

    Adult writes her name, age, and date on the back.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Make one every birthday.
  • Add parent handprints on a larger canvas.
  • Use different colors for different family members.
  • Photograph her hand next to the finished print.
🧪 Color Mixing Laboratory15–30 min · Medium mess · 5 store items ⌄

A simple first experiment: two colors go in, a new color comes out.

BUY AT MICHAELS

FROM HOME

  • Water cup
  • Paper towel
  • Old spoon for parent portioning

DO THIS

  1. 1

    Put tiny puddles of red, yellow, and blue in separate wells.

  2. 2

    Make a second empty mixing area.

  3. 3

    Let her choose two colors and mix them together.

  4. 4

    Paint the new color on paper.

  5. 5

    Repeat with other pairs and then add white to make lighter versions.

EXTRA IDEAS

  • Make a page labeled with each color recipe.
  • Ask her to predict the new color before mixing.
  • Write her funny names for the colors on the back.
  • Use the mixed colors to paint a wooden shape afterward.
HOME SETUP

Make “yes” easy.

Keep everyday supplies reachable and the messy/special stuff parent-controlled.

🧺 Everyday caddy

  • 6–8 washable broad markers
  • 4 jumbo crayons
  • 2–3 Twistables
  • 1 glue stick
  • Small drawing pad

⚡ Pick by energy level

  • 5 minutes: markers + drawing pad.
  • Low mess: dot markers + stickers.
  • Paint day: 3–5 tempera colors + one interesting surface.
  • Weekend: wooden figures, tape-resist canvas, or cardboard house.
  • Outside: giant mural or nature printing.

🖼️ Keep the best

Date the back of good pieces. Keep a slim portfolio, photograph 3D projects, and rotate a few current favorites on display instead of saving absolutely everything.

🚫 Skip for now

Loose glitter, beads, sequins, tiny pom-poms, tiny googly eyes, tiny buttons, magnets, craft knives, hot glue, and anything labeled above her age.
